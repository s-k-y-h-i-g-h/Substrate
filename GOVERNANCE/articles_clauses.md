# Articles of Association — Key Clauses for Substrate CIC

For custom articles filed alongside (or instead of) model articles. These are the *non-standard* clauses Substrate adds. Standard CIC model articles govern the rest.

---

## 1. Director Remuneration

**1.1** The company shall be permitted to pay directors (or any of them) remuneration for services rendered to the company, including salaries, fees, expenses, and benefits in kind.

**1.2** Any payment of director remuneration shall require a resolution of the board, recorded in writing, stating the amount and rationale, and shall be disclosed publicly in the annual report.

**1.3** No director shall determine their own remuneration; at least one non-interested director must approve each remuneration decision.

**Rationale:** CICs are permitted to pay directors, but the CIC Regulator expects transparency and caps. This clause permits modest self-remuneration (critical for a founder on disability benefits) while maintaining governance discipline.

---

## 2. Conflicts of Interest

**2.1** A director who has a direct or indirect financial or personal interest in a proposed transaction or arrangement with the company must declare the nature and extent of that interest to the board before any decision is taken.

**2.2** A director with a declared interest may speak and vote on the matter, provided the declaration is minuted.

**Rationale:** Permits founder involvement in procurement/sourcing decisions (which are unavoidable in a small operation) while maintaining disclosure. Standard CIC articles are silent on conflicts — this adds it explicitly.

---

## 3. Asset Lock

**3.1** The company is a Community Interest Company and holds its assets for the community purposes set out in its objects clause.

**3.2** If the company is dissolved, its assets shall not be distributed among its members but shall be transferred to another body whose objects are similar to those of the company (an "asset-lock recipient"), to be determined by the board or, failing agreement, by the CIC Regulator.

**Rationale:** Redundant with CIC law but explicit in articles adds clarity for donors and grant-makers reviewing governance. Defines the "what happens if it folds" scenario.

---

## 4. Dissolution — Asset Lock Recipient

**4.1** The intended asset-lock recipient is a body with objects to relieve poverty, advance health, or advance education through open-access nutritional and pharmacological resources, as determined by the board at the time of dissolution.

**4.2** If no such body can be identified, assets shall pass to a registered UK charity with similar objects selected by the Charity Commission.

---

## 5. Open Data Default

**5.1** All non-personal operational data produced by the company — procurement records, distribution volumes, protocol version history, adverse event summaries, financial summaries — shall be published in machine-readable form under an open licence (CC-BY-4.0 or equivalent) unless doing so would infringe data protection law or compromise the safety of recipients or partners.

**Rationale:** This is the "open-source" guarantee at the governance level. It makes the protocol a public good, not proprietary IP.

---

## 6. Membership

**6.1** Membership of the company shall be open to any person who supports the objects of the company and agrees to be bound by these articles.

**6.2** Each member shall have one vote at general meetings.

**6.3** Minimum membership: three (3) persons.

**Rationale:** CIC limited by guarantee requires at least 3 members (who are also directors on incorporation). This clause allows expansion while maintaining democratic accountability.

---

## Template sourcing

These clauses are adapted from the UK CIC Association custom articles template and the Community Interest Company (Model Community Interest Company) Regulations 2013 (SI 2013/2423). Review by a solicitor with CIC experience is recommended before filing.
